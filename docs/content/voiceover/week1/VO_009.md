# VO_009: Moving your stop 'just this once'
Written by: Claude Opus 5.5

## Narration
"I'll just move my stop a little." Here's what that does. You planned to risk fifty dollars. The trade goes against you. You move the stop. Now you're risking a hundred. Then a hundred and fifty. That's three times your plan. And your journal still says you had a stop. Moving a stop further away isn't managing the trade. It's changing the plan after the plan stopped working. If you do it, tag it. Moved stop. Then check what it cost you. Be honest in the comments. Have you ever moved a stop?

## Delivery notes
- Say the opening line in a light, casual "inner voice" tone, as if quoting yourself. Then drop to your normal calm voice for "Here's what that does."
- Slow down through the numbers. Small pause before each one: "fifty dollars", "a hundred", "a hundred and fifty". Each should feel heavier than the last.
- Stress "three times". Then a one-beat pause before "And your journal still says you had a stop." Say that line drily, not mocking.
- Stress "isn't" and "changing". This is the core idea, so keep it slow and clear.
- "Moved stop." is the tag name. Say it as a short label, then a beat.
- End warm and open on the question. Not a challenge, an invitation.
